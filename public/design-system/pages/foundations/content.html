Language

  • UK English: colour, organise, centre, programme (but "program" for software).
  • Plain words, short sentences. Say what something does, not how clever it is.
  • Avoid em dashes in copy. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon.
  • Sentence case for everything: headings, buttons, tabs.

Dates, times and money

  • Times are Sydney time. Say so once (for example in the hero) rather than on every time.
  • Dates: "Sat 9 Oct", "8 to 14 Oct". No ordinals, no leading zeros.
  • Times: "7:30pm", "10am".
  • Money in AUD: "$25", "From $120". Add "AUD" only where visitors from overseas might read another dollar.

Labels

  • A button says what happens: "Add to your plan", "Save & share".
  • A link that moves the page says where it goes: "See Where to watch", "Open your plan".
  • Short visible labels can carry fuller hidden text for screen readers.

Toasts

A short title saying what the person did, then one line naming only what changed. If nothing needed to change, say so. At most one link, named for where it goes. See toast.

Neutrality

DNSW is a government agency: no business is favoured, there are no bookings on site, and every listing gets a one-line reason it fits ("Open Saturday, free, 10 min from the stadium"), not a sales line.

Not an AI itinerary

No chat box, no "Generate with AI", no sparkle icons and no long generated paragraphs. Everything is a card, a time or a map.
