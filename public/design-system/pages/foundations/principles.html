1. Reward early, then build on it

Give people something useful as soon as possible and let each extra input improve it, rather than holding results back until a form is finished. Reveal options and detail as they become relevant, not all up front.

A trip planner shows suggestions from the first choice (a region, a date or an interest) and refines them as more is added.

Open with a long questionnaire and show nothing until it is complete.

  • Search and listing pages show results straight away, with filters alongside.
  • Product and event pages lead with what helps someone decide (when, where, price, why go) and keep the fine print further down.

2. Don't make people do the work

Ask only for what the next step needs. Prefer sensible defaults, inferring from what people have already done, and asking later over asking now. Make an input optional whenever the result still works without it.

Let people save a plan without an account. Ask them to sign in only when they want to keep or share it.

Ask again for a region or date picked on the page before.

3. Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA

AA, not AAA. Controls are at least 24px to tap, and 36px is the usual button height. Tap areas are not padded out to 44px. See accessibility.

4. White space groups what belongs together

One spacing rhythm on the 4px scale, so distance shows how things relate: tight inside a component, 24px between parts of one family, 48px between families, and the largest break after the hero. Prefer space to boxes and dividers. See layout.

Product principles for the planner

  1. Matches are fixed; the free time around them is what the fan plans.
  2. Real, neutral listings only, each with a one-line reason it fits.
  3. The fan decides. Nothing is added to a plan without them choosing it.
  4. A plan leaves the site easily: copy, print, download or share, no account.
