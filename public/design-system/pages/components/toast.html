When to use

When something a person does changes a part of the page they cannot see from where they are: another section, their plan, what is behind a sheet. Ask of every new or moved control: what does it change beyond itself, and can the person see that?

When not to use

  • When the change already shows where they acted: a pick that offers Undo, a marker settling on the plan, dates showing under the slider being dragged.
  • When the toast would sit over the control the person is still using. Leave it out.

Content

  • A short title saying what they did.
  • One line naming what it changed, listing only the parts that changed. If nothing needed to change, say so.
  • A toast that repeats as someone adjusts something is title only, with the change in the title ("Your days are now 14 Oct to 6 Nov"), and updates in place.
  • At most one link, named for where it goes ("See Where to watch", "Open your plan"). No link inside a sheet that would open something over that sheet.

Behaviour

  • One at a time; a new one replaces the old.
  • Stays 6 seconds, longer while hovered or focused. A cross closes it.
  • Background 90% opaque. Rises and fades in; reduced motion fades only.
  • Not a live region when screen readers already hear the change.

Position

  • 16px from the bottom on a phone, 24px on a desktop.
  • 12px above whatever is pinned to the bottom and showing (a bar, a sheet's bottom row, the keyboard), and it follows them as they appear or go.
  • On a desktop, one row with the link beside the text, up to 520px wide.
