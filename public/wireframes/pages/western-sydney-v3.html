About Sydney West

Western Sydney isn’t the drive-through before Sydney starts — it’s 25 to 95 kilometres of Sydney most people never stop in.

PLACEHOLDER — needs a verified surprising stat or comparison that challenges the “nothing to see out west” assumption (e.g. how a Western Sydney food strip, park or attraction compares to a well-known equivalent). Insert only once confirmed by research — do not publish an invented figure.

Spanning Parramatta, Penrith, Campbelltown, Blacktown, Camden and the Southern Highlands fringe, 25–95km west of the CBD, the region is home to Sydney Olympic Park, Featherdale Wildlife Park and the NSW Rail Museum — and opens direct via Western Sydney International Airport from October 2026.

Ask someone who’s never driven past Parramatta Road what’s out west, and most guess wrong. The locals who do know already treat it as theirs — close enough for a Tuesday, good enough to skip the CBD for on a Saturday.

Sydney West Neighbourhoods

Nine neighbourhoods most CBD locals have driven past without stopping.

Parramatta

Sandstone walls, riverside lawns and a food scene that spills onto the street — Sydney's second city has been layering history since colonial settlement, and it still shows.

Known for: history and heritage; multicultural food scene

Highlights:

  • Explore the rich heritage of Old Government House, Australia's oldest surviving public building.
  • Wander the UNESCO-listed riverside parklands of Parramatta Park.
  • Catch a show at Riverside Theatres, home to 700+ performances a year, from comedy to concerts.

Riverside Theatres alone stages 700+ performances a year — proof the crowds already show up, whether the rest of Sydney notices or not.

Cabramatta

Street food smoke drifts from the stalls of Cabramatta Markets before you're even through the ornate Pai Lau Friendship Arch — Sydney's home of Vietnamese and South-East Asian food and culture.

Known for: Vietnamese food scene; Asian grocery markets

Highlights:

  • Snap a photo beneath the ornate Pai Lau Friendship Arch, the gateway to Cabramatta.
  • Take in the sights and sounds of Freedom Plaza, the heart of the neighbourhood.
  • Browse the stalls of Cabramatta Markets for fresh produce and street food.
Learn more about Cabramatta →

Sydney Olympic Park

Sydney Olympic Park still hums with 2000 Olympics history — the roar of the crowd fills Accor Stadium on game night, while by day it's wetland boardwalks and cycling trails through Bicentennial Park beside the Parramatta River.

Known for: major stadiums; wetlands and cycling trails

Highlights:

  • Catch a match or concert at Accor Stadium, the centrepiece of the 2000 Olympics.
  • Cycle or picnic across the wetlands and trails of Bicentennial Park.
  • Check out a show or event at Sydney Showground.
Learn more about Sydney Olympic Park →

Blacktown

Native wildlife rustles within arm's reach at Featherdale Wildlife Park, game nights fill Blacktown Showground with noise, and this fast-growing multicultural hub anchors one of Sydney's biggest regional shopping precincts.

Known for: Featherdale Wildlife Park; live sport at Blacktown Stadium

Highlights:

  • Get up close with native wildlife at Featherdale Wildlife Park.
  • Catch a game or event at Blacktown Showground.
  • Enjoy a walk or picnic among the bushland and lakes of Nurragingy Reserve.
Learn more about Blacktown →

Campbelltown

Gravel crunches underfoot on the trails through Australia's largest botanic garden, contemporary art and Indigenous heritage share space at Campbelltown Arts Centre, and the still water of the Japanese Gardens slows the afternoon right down.

Known for: history and heritage; live entertainment

Highlights:

  • Discover contemporary art and Indigenous heritage at Campbelltown Arts Centre.
  • Stroll the tranquil Japanese Gardens.
  • Explore Australia's largest botanic garden at the Australian Botanical Garden.
Learn more about Campbelltown →

Penrith

Free-fall roars through iFLY Downunder, whitewater churns at Penrith Whitewater Stadium, and the Nepean River runs calm just beyond both — all within an hour's drive from Sydney, at the foot of the Blue Mountains.

Known for: indoor skydiving, whitewater adventure and Blue Mountains access

Highlights:

  • Feel the rush of indoor skydiving at iFLY Downunder.
  • Take on the rapids at Penrith Whitewater Stadium.
  • Relax or cycle along the Nepean River foreshore.
Learn more about Penrith →

Thirlmere

The hiss and clang of heritage locomotives rolls through the NSW Rail Museum; just beyond it, the trails of Thirlmere Lakes National Park run quiet enough to hear the bush instead.

Known for: NSW Rail Museum; heritage steam train rides

Highlights:

  • Step back in time among the heritage locomotives at the NSW Rail Museum.
  • Walk the quiet trails of Thirlmere Lakes National Park.
  • Catch the heritage steam trains in action at the seasonal Steam Fest.
Learn more about Thirlmere →

Appin

Bushland trails follow the Georges River past historic farmland, opening out at Appin Lookout for sweeping views over the valley below.

Known for: bushwalking trails; rural heritage

Highlights:

  • Explore the bushland trails along the Georges River.
  • Take in sweeping views from Appin Lookout.
  • Visit the historic Cataract Dam.
Learn more about Appin →

Camden

Heritage cottages and working paddocks still edge Camden Park House & Garden, with local vineyards pouring tastings just down the road — Sydney's colonial farming heartland now sits on the doorstep of the new Western Sydney Airport precinct.

Known for: colonial heritage; vineyards and farm gates

Highlights:

  • Tour the colonial-era Camden Park House & Garden.
  • Sample a drop at one of Camden's local vineyards.
  • Join the community for the seasonal Camden Show.
Learn more about Camden →

A Perfect Saturday in Western Sydney

One loop, five neighbourhoods, zero CBD traffic.

  1. 9:00am

    Sydney Olympic Park

    Start slow while it's quiet — a wetland walk or cycle through Bicentennial Park before the day heats up.

  2. 11:00am

    Parramatta

    Wander the sandstone rooms of Old Government House, then cut across the riverside lawns of Parramatta Park.

  3. 1:00pm

    Cabramatta

    Lunch is whatever smells best at Cabramatta Markets, eaten somewhere near the ornate Pai Lau Friendship Arch.

  4. 3:30pm

    Penrith

    Pick your adrenaline: the rapids at Penrith Whitewater Stadium, or the free-fall rush of iFLY Downunder.

  5. 7:00pm

    Parramatta, again

    Loop back for dinner and one of Riverside Theatres' 700+ annual shows before the drive home.
